AdaGCN uses a shared encoder for source and target graphs, trains a discriminator with gradient penalty on the learned embeddings, and optimizes source classification plus domain alignment.

ADGFN now keeps that AdaGCN training structure intact and changes only two things:
1. After the shared AdaGCN encoder, it applies a separate learnable monomial graph filter to each domain.
2. It adds an adversarial probe-distribution game so the source and target filters are trained to align the pushforward of probe features, while the probe distributions try to make that alignment harder.

In short, AdaGCN learns domain-invariant embeddings with adversarial alignment. ADGFN keeps the same backbone, classifier, discriminator, and gradient-penalty workflow, but gives each domain its own learnable post-encoder filter and regularizes those filters through adversarial probe matching.
